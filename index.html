How to play
  • Move with the arrow keys or WASD (on a phone, the D-pad). Tap a direction to turn on the spot.
  • Talk to anyone with a ! using Space or A. They check you in to real ASPIRE quests. In-person quests only work at their building.
  • Abilities are the icons above the map. Each unlocks when a capability in your skill tree reaches an HCS level, for example running shoes at Initiative & Drive L1 and the map at Problem Solving & Data L1.
  • Challenges hide in Victoria Park's long grass. Your capabilities are your moves.
  • Classmates in your room code appear live. Walk up and press Space to wave or squad up. Checking in together earns a squad bonus.
  • The campus is built from OpenStreetMap data, so buildings and paths are where they really are.

How the model works

The brief asks the Recognition & Progression working group to "design the ASPIRE recognition framework, including points, badges, awards and/or other mechanisms that recognise participation and capability development. Define achievement criteria, progression thresholds, evidence requirements and how recognition connects to the Employability Portfolio." The S1 2027 scope: "a simple, transparent recognition model that can mature over time."

The one-sentence version: showing up earns you XP, which recognises participation; proving what you learned levels up a capability on a five-step evidence ladder, which recognises development; and every level from 2 up flows straight into your Employability Portfolio.

1 · Two currencies, kept separate

XP: participation

Earned by checking in to ASPIRE activities (QR or Canvas). Drives your rank and the cohort goal. Easy to earn, so it rewards showing up.

Capability levels: development

Can't be bought with XP. Each level needs evidence. This stops the system from rewarding just turning up and keeps it credible to employers.

Badges: milestones

Auto-awarded when published rules are met: breadth, firsts and helping others. They give you something to aim for and something to share.

2 · The skill tree = Human Capability Standards

The tree follows the Human Capability Standards Reference Framework, Education Version 4 (2025) from the Institute for Working Futures. It has 12 durable capabilities in three domains, and each capability has four skills and four mindsets. Every node in the tree shows the official HCS definition, its skills and mindsets, and the game abilities it unlocks. The LEAD prestige branch uses the HCS L.E.A.D. leadership domain, which the education version leaves out.

Levels = HCS proficiency levels

HCS describes capability across seven levels of autonomy, responsibility and complexity, from Follow to Shape. A degree realistically covers Levels 1–5, so the game's five levels map directly to them. Levels 6 (Improve) and 7 (Shape) come with professional practice after graduation.

6 · MVP (S1 2027) vs dream scenario

MVP: use what already exists

  • Quest check-ins via QR codes and Canvas modules
  • XP, ranks and badges calculated from published rules (like this prototype)
  • Reflection and endorsement forms in Canvas, with staff verification at existing events
  • Portfolio feed as an exportable record
  • Core tree only (the 12 HCS Education capabilities in HEAD, HEART and HANDS)

Dream: mature over time

  • LEAD prestige branch unlocked
  • Verified levels issued as Open Badges / verifiable credentials, shareable to LinkedIn
  • Employer-set quests and employers verifying Level 4 directly
  • Campus world with live presence at real events (this prototype shows the idea) and moderation tools
  • Analytics that link participation to SES and graduate outcomes

8 · Questions for the strategy session

  1. Where are we now? What gets recognised in ASPIRE today, and what goes unseen?
  2. Which incentive would actually change what you do: XP, badges, squads, or portfolio credentials?
  3. Is a 50-word reflection the right bar for Level 2? What would stop people gaming it?
  4. Who should be able to verify Level 4, and how do we make that easy for employers?
  5. How do we make sure students who work 20+ hours a week aren't left behind?
